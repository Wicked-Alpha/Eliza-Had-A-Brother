# Corrections

Claims found in published accounts of ELIZA, PARRY and their 1972 meeting, set against the record. Each correction links to the exhibit that carries the evidence. The section below the table says where each claim appears.

| # | The claim | What the record shows | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PARRY and ELIZA talked at the ICCC conference in Washington, October 1972. | RFC 439 dates the session to 18 September 1972, 36 days before ICCC opened, run from UCLA. The ICCC booklet lists PARRY and the DOCTOR as separate hands-on demos. | [01](exhibits/01-rfc-439/), [07](exhibits/07-iccc-1972/) |
| 2 | PARRY talked to Weizenbaum's ELIZA at MIT. | The DOCTOR ran on BBN's TENEX system, and the code evidence ties it to Bernie Cosell's Lisp version. The 1972 ICCC booklet did credit it to Weizenbaum by name. | [03](exhibits/03-cosell-doctor/) |
| 3 | 33 psychiatrists could pick out PARRY only 48 percent of the time. | The 33 psychiatrists rated how paranoid the transcripts were. The 48 percent is 32 of 67 ACM computer scientists. Psychiatrists asked to pick the machine got 21 of 41, or 51 percent. | [06](exhibits/06-psychiatrist-tests/) |
| 4 | PARRY was written in 1972. | It was described in a paper accepted on 4 January 1971, after 25 psychiatrists had already interviewed it. | [05](exhibits/05-parry-1971/) |
| 5 | ELIZA was written in Lisp. | ELIZA was written in MAD-SLIP. The Lisp DOCTOR was Cosell's, at BBN. | [04](exhibits/04-eliza-1965/), [03](exhibits/03-cosell-doctor/) |
| 6 | PARRY was written in plain LISP. | It was written in MLISP, which translates into LISP 1.6. The surviving source also includes PDP-10 assembly. | [05](exhibits/05-parry-1971/) |
| 7 | RFC 439 dates from 1972. | The RFC is dated 21 January 1973. The session it records is from 1972. | [01](exhibits/01-rfc-439/) |
| 8 | PARRY simulated paranoid schizophrenia. | Colby's 1971 paper used "paranoid" for a general mode of thinking and noted that clinicians agree poorly on subcategories such as paranoid schizophrenia. | [05](exhibits/05-parry-1971/) |
| 9 | Colby adapted ELIZA into PARRY. | PARRY was a separate program, written in MLISP, that played a patient where ELIZA's best-known script played a therapist. Colby's 1973 draft preface credits Sylvia Weber Russell with writing the original version. | [05](exhibits/05-parry-1971/) |

## Where these claims appear

- **Wikipedia's PARRY article**, checked in October 2026: the ICCC setting (1), PARRY written in 1972 (4), LISP (6), a reference to RFC 439 dated 21 January 1972 (7), paranoid schizophrenia (8), and the 33 psychiatrists with 48 percent (3).
- **The Computer History Museum's 1970s internet history timeline:** a conversation at ICCC between PARRY and ELIZA, which it places at MIT (1 and 2).
- **The ICCC booklet itself, October 1972:** it calls the DOCTOR a LISP program written by Joseph Weizenbaum (2 and 5). The team that reanimated the original ELIZA describes the belief that ELIZA was written in Lisp as a long-standing misunderstanding across the community (5).
- **Smithsonian Magazine, 15 January 2026:** Colby adapted ELIZA into a program called PARRY (9).

These sources are useful. They repeat a story that the documents tell differently.

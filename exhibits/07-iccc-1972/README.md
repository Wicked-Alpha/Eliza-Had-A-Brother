# Exhibit 07: ICCC, Washington, October 1972

The International Conference on Computer Communication ran from 24 to 26 October 1972 at the Washington Hilton. Bob Kahn of BBN ran the ARPANET demonstration that went with it, and Vint Cerf spent March to October 1972 helping organize it. It is where many retellings place the PARRY and DOCTOR conversation.

## What the booklet shows

The booklet prepared for attendees is *Scenarios for using the ARPANET at the International Conference on Computer Communication*, NIC 11863, with a preface by Bob Metcalfe dated 24 October 1972. It gives step-by-step scenarios for programs attendees could try for themselves at the terminals.

- **BBN DOCTOR**, on BBN-TENEX, Host #69. The booklet credits it to Joseph Weizenbaum, calls it a LISP program, and ties it to his 1966 paper. Its sample session ends with a bill of $7.15.
- **SAIL PARRY**, on SAIL, Host #11. The booklet describes it as a program by Ken Colby of Stanford that simulates a paranoid patient in a mental hospital, and asks the user to play the psychiatrist.

They are listed as two separate hands-on scenarios.

## What this means for the story

The only 1972 conversation between PARRY and the DOCTOR that this archive has found documented is the session in [RFC 439](../01-rfc-439/), dated 18 September 1972, 36 days before the conference opened, and run from UCLA. This archive has found no primary source that places a conversation between the two programs at ICCC.

If you have a primary source showing PARRY and the DOCTOR talking to each other at ICCC, such as a 1972 press report, a conference program or a participant's account, please open an issue.

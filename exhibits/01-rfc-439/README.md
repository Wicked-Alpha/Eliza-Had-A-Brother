## How to read it

- Lines that start with `*` are PARRY.
- Unmarked lines in the conversation are the DOCTOR.
- Lines that start with `comment:` are comments added to the transcript, presumably by Cerf. There are eight of them.

The DOCTOR opens by asking for the user's problems and asking that each input end with a period or a question mark. PARRY says people get on its nerves, then moves to the track at Bay Meadows, crooked gambling, a bookie who did not pay off, the underworld, and the MAFIA. It complains that the DOCTOR keeps asking the same questions. When PARRY says goodbye, the DOCTOR hands it a bill for $399.29.

## The machines in the footer

The last lines of the RFC say PARRY was running at SAIL and the DOCTOR at BBN Tenex, both accessed from UCLA/SEX.

- **SAIL** was the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory's computer. The ICCC booklet of October 1972 lists it as ARPANET Host #11.
- **BBN-TENEX** was a host at BBN, listed in the same booklet as Host #69. Which DOCTOR program it was running is covered in [Exhibit 03](../03-cosell-doctor/).
- **UCLA/SEX:** the October 1972 ICCC booklet says UCLA-NMC ran an experimental system called SEX on its XDS Sigma-7. RFC 597 (December 1973) lists UCLA-NMC as ARPANET host 1, a Sigma 7 running SEX. In a 1990 oral history, Cerf recalled the name as short for Sigma Experimental. Treat that expansion as his recollection.

## What the record does not say

RFC 439 does not say who typed, who was watching, or how the output of one program was passed to the other. This archive does not guess.

For where the opening "Good Evening." and the mid-session "Excuse me for just a minute" most likely came from, see [Exhibit 03](../03-cosell-doctor/).

## Where Cerf was

By his own account, Cerf helped organize the ICCC demonstration from March to October 1972 while still at UCLA, and moved to Stanford in October 1972. The RFC, dated January 1973, gives his affiliation as SU-ERL and does not expand it.
